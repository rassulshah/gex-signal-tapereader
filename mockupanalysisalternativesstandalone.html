Analysis Tab Alternatives

Five ways to organise the same evidence. Each is drawn as the tab would look, then judged on one question: does it change what you do at the next tap? The three-moment layout from the last mockup is the baseline they are measured against.

What it is good atBrowsable. Every study has a home you can find by its subject; refused work sits beside shipped work. This is the notebook the doctrine tells you to keep.
What it costsIt is a catalogue, not an instrument — nothing on it is about now. Kings, setups and deflections overlap (a King tap is a deflection is a setup), so the same study wants two homes.
Right as the evidence layer under any other layout; wrong as the whole tab, because it answers "what do we know" and never "what should I expect at this tap."

The recommendation

Keep the three-moment layout as the tab, and take one thing from each alternative. The expectation card in ② becomes the setup grader (C) once its lines are measured, and it looks its numbers up in the conditions matrix (D), which is what the nightly computes anyway. The after-the-close moment ③ is drawn as the session timeline (E). The evidence rows under each moment are the research notebook (A). And the doctrine scorecard (B) goes to the Testing tab beside the register, where a row of claims with verdicts belongs.
