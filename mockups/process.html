The five-step tape process

A futures day-trading process built around what the panel actually knows — and a step indicator that replaces Skylit's, because their five steps describe their methodology, not your trade.

Why two data sources, and what each is for

The whole argument for having both InsiderFinance and Skylit is that they answer different questions, and neither supports the full process alone.

SourceWhat it isChanges
chain The terrain. Open interest × gamma — where exposure sits. Walls, zero gamma, magnet, max pain, expected move. Set before the open. OI is fixed intraday; only greeks re-weight.
tape The flow. Live dealer positioning that accumulates and dissipates. Nodes growing, rolling, being defended or abandoned. Continuously. A node grew 18M → 600M in one session.

InsiderFinance alone cannot tell you whether a level is being defended right now. It's a pre-market planning tool — a map of the day's terrain. It has no view of what dealers did in the last ten minutes.

Skylit alone cannot frame the day. Its values are flow, so they have no fixed structure to anchor to — which is exactly why our levels disagreed with theirs until we understood they were measuring different things.

The chain gives you the plan. The tape tells you when to act on it.

The five steps

01

FRAME

What kind of day is this?

Before any trade: the box and the playbook. Regime decides the style — positive gamma means fade the extremes, negative gamma means moves extend and you follow breaks. Expected move decides whether a target is even reachable.

Panel
Direction header — REG: −G, TARGET, EM ±28; the ladder's outer bounds; voids
Source
chain
Advance
You know the box, the regime, and how much range the day is priced for
02

BIAS

Which side of the box am I playing?

Not a prediction — a lean that decides which levels you're interested in. In a down bias you only care about rallies into resistance; the support levels become targets, not entries.

Panel
Verdict, four inputs, evidence line, coiling direction
Source
both — tape for trend/drift, chain for DEX/skew
Advance
A lean exists. No lean is a valid answer — it means step 3 never arrives
Weakest link. One session of data, and the direction factors scored at or below base rate. Treat the bias as the least reliable thing on the panel and let structure carry more weight.
03

LOCATION

Is price anywhere worth trading?

The discipline step, and the one that saves the most money. No trades in a void. Price mid-gap has nothing to lean on, so there's no defined risk — you wait for it to reach a level.

Panel
Ladder with price in position; void shading; distance to the nearest actionable row
Source
both
Advance
Price is at a level marked ENTRY — not near it, at it
Missing: reachability. An entry 21 points away with 9 points of expected move left will never trigger today, and the panel currently presents it as though it might.
04

REACTION

Is this level being defended?

The step only Skylit can serve, and the reason the tape is worth having. A level is just a price until you see what dealers do when it's tested. Accumulating into the touch means they're defending. Dissipating means they're standing aside and it will break.

Panel
Node state acm/dec; roll direction and count; test count and whether it held
Source
tape
Advance
The node is accumulating, or has rolled toward price — dealers are showing up
Missing: the trigger. Nothing on the panel says "price just touched 7690 and turned." That is the moment you act, and it's currently silent.
05

EXECUTE

Where's my risk, and where do I get out?

Entry at the level, invalidation beyond the next level — structural, not a fixed point stop. First exit at the next ladder row; let it run through a void, take something into a cluster.

Panel
→ 7676 (19) · ✗ 7716 (21) · 0.9R; the ladder as the exit map; PATH waypoints as intermediate stops
Source
chain for targets, tape for the trailing structure
Exit early
The level fails, or drift diverges from the bias — the idea is dead either way

The step indicator

Skylit's Steps 1 2 3 4 5 describes their methodology. Ours would show which step you are on right now, derived from panel state — so the tool doesn't just hold data, it tells you where you are in your own process.

The honest summary. Steps 1, 3 and 5 are well served today — framing, location and risk are all structural, all objective, all checkable against the chart. Step 4 is where the panel is unique and also where it's incomplete: it can tell you a node is accumulating but not that price just turned on it. Step 2 is the weakest and should carry the least weight until the scorecard says otherwise.
