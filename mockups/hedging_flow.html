HEDGING FLOW — CAN IT CARRY PRICE TO THE EXPECTED MOVE?

Your idea, made concrete. Net GEX is already denominated in dollars per 1% move, and 1% of 7674 is 76.74 points — so dealers must trade $214M for every single point to stay neutral. That is the flow. Whether it helps or fights the move is decided entirely by the gamma regime.

THE MECHANISM

NEGATIVE GAMMA — the flow FEEDS the move

price ticks up → dealers get short delta
→ they must BUY to re-hedge
→ that buying lifts price further
→ and it starts again
Self-feeding. Reaching the expected high needs $8.3B of hedging — and the hedging is pushing that way. This is what a trend day is made of.

POSITIVE GAMMA — the flow FIGHTS it

price ticks up → dealers get long delta
→ they must SELL to re-hedge
→ that selling caps price
→ and the move stalls
Self-damping. That same $8.3B is now resistance to overcome, not a tailwind. This is why levels hold and fades work here.

A chip that says FEEDS when hedging is actually braking is worse than no chip at all: it is a wrong number with a DIRECTION attached, on the face, under our own name. Same class as the truncated Zero Gamma, with more consequence. The reconciliation is its own small piece of work — compare our per-expiry netGEX against their published figure on matched aggregation and find where the convention diverges.

Two further limits, stated up front. Notional is not impact: $8.3B of hedging spread across a session is not obviously decisive, and I have no measurement of how much range it actually explains. And GEX is a snapshot — the per-point figure drifts as price moves through strikes, so it describes the move it is currently in, not the one ahead.

It would be enrolled, not trusted: "does a high hedging-flow-per-point precede reaching the expected move — split by regime?" Non-voting until n≥20, like everything else.
