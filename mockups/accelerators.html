The regime chip reads −G (negative gamma, hedging amplifies). But FLIP is 7664.89 and price is 7691.75 — price is ABOVE the gamma flip, which by definition is positive gamma territory. The face is asserting both at once.

It is not necessarily a bug: regime2D reads Skylit's gamma book on the SPY tape, while FLIP is InsiderFinance's published Zero Gamma from the SPX chain. Two different books, two underlyings, two expiry sets — they are allowed to differ. But when they disagree about which side of the flip you are on, marking accelerators and brakes becomes a coin toss: the same level is a brake under one book and an accelerator under the other.

This has to be settled first, and it is the same open question as the netGEX sign gap (ours −$16.41B, theirs +$13.30B). One reconciliation answers both. Until then this is a good design sitting on an unreliable polarity, and polarity is the entire point of it.
