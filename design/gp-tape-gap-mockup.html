One rule, both rails: the % starts N characters after the strike

You asked for the two strips to be spaced the same. That makes it one number, not two — and the number has to honour both directions you gave: the SPX gap wider by one, the SPY gap narrower. N = 2 characters is the value that does both. The gaps below are literal space characters at Helvetica 10, the plugin's own font, so you can count them.

What the rule changes

todayeach strip places the % at its own fixed column offset — colW/2 on the SPY strip, flush against the pane edge on the SPX strip. Two different rules, so the two strips can never look alike, and neither gap is a number anyone chose: it is whatever is left after the strike text.
proposedone rule for both: the % starts exactly N character-spaces after the strike text ends, N measured with getTextWidth(" ") at that strip's own font. Same N on both rails, so they match at any Font size.
what you give upon the SPX strip the percentages are right-justified at the pane edge today, so their right edges line up. Measuring from the left instead means −100% and +4% no longer end flush — the right edge goes ragged, as it already is on the SPY strip. That is the price of the two strips matching; there is no way to have both.
one placegpl::tapeCols(font, spaceW, strikeW, nChars) in the logic header, called by both rails, pinned by Gate B — today the offsets are computed inline in the render, differently per side.
Say "build 2" or "build 1" and it ships as GP 0.73. If the ragged right edge on the SPX strip bothers you once you see it live, the fix is one number and one line.
