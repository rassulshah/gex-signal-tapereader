Your two examples, drawn the way I would build them. The arrow is the roll; the rows show why it fired.

Ceiling rolled down — your first screenshot

▲ call-dominant ▼ put-dominant bands: Skylit flow · dashed: IF levels90m
CEIL ROLL ↓ 7716 → 7705−71% / +64% over 30mshadow · not voting

7716 thins as 7705 thickens. The dying row fades to almost nothing on the right; the new one starts faint and brightens. That is the whole signal — mass moving between strikes — and the arrow just names it.

Floor rolled up — your second screenshot

▲ call-dominant ▼ put-dominant bands: Skylit flow · dashed: IF levels90m
FLOOR ROLL ↑ 7674 → 7685−63% / +72% over 30mshadow · not voting

Arrow colour is the implication, not the polarity. Red for a ceiling coming down, green for a floor coming up — deliberately different from the purple/yellow of the bands, which mean put/call.

"shadow · not voting" is on the face on purpose. On the four archived days this pattern had no measurable edge, and every one of those days was a down day, so nothing could be proven either way. It draws and records; it does not push the BIAS call until it earns it.
