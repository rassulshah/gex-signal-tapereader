# Heatseeker Patterns (skylit.ai/learn/heatseeker-patterns) — captured 2026-08-13

RULE ZERO: Patterns don't override magnitude. Same setup on a thin node vs a king node trades differently. **Magnitude overrides pattern.**

## The Rug Setup (BEARISH)
Structure: POSITIVE gamma stacks ABOVE spot. NEGATIVE gamma sits BELOW spot. Spot is positioned BELOW the positive node.
Mechanics: the positive gamma above acts as a CEILING (dealers long gamma sell into rallies, capping the move). Below spot the negative gamma REMOVES THE FLOOR — dealer hedges pro-cyclical, price falls -> dealers sell -> decline feeds on itself.
Mental model: a ceiling that not only blocks price but pushes it down faster once rejected. Rejection WITH acceleration.
Trade: directional — ride the acceleration AFTER the rejection confirms (not the rejection itself).
Watch for it: on the approach to a LARGE positive node when negative gamma sits within the current session's expected range. "That combination is when the setup has teeth."

## The Reverse Rug (BULLISH — exact mirror)
Structure: NEGATIVE gamma ABOVE spot. POSITIVE gamma BELOW spot. Spot sits ABOVE the positive node.
Mechanics: positive gamma below DEFLECTS price back up (dealers buy dips = mechanical floor). Negative gamma above AMPLIFIES the bounce (pro-cyclical to the upside). Support WITH pro-cyclical momentum. Sharp reversal, not slow grind.
Higher-conviction bullish: both forces aligned same direction (support below + acceleration fuel above).

## Pika Clouds (= our "Cluster")
DENSE CLUSTERS of POSITIVE gamma nodes (NOT a single king node). A REGION with MULTIPLE positive nodes in close proximity -> overlapping contrarian dealer flow across a range of strikes.
Behavior: trend HALTS, movement inefficient, price STICKS/ROTATES/CHOPS. Rejection probability elevated at each node. Acts like a GRAVITY WELL.
Not bullish/bearish by default: above spot = resistance, below = support, surrounding spot = inefficient range (false moves both ways).
MAGNITUDE MATTERS MOST HERE: thin cloud = soft/porous; dense king-level cloud = can pin all session. **Check exposure scale before trading around it.**
NOTE: Pika = POSITIVE gamma specifically. (Purple/negative dense region is a different animal — Barney.)

## The Beach Ball (= overshoot/reversion, NOT a breakout)
Price OVERSHOOTS a significant POSITIVE node, punches through, then REACTS/reverts. "Like pushing a beach ball underwater — deeper it goes, stronger the release."
NOT a breakout — it's a STRETCH, not a break. The node is absorbing, not failing. Common mistake: chasing the break.
Identify in real time: FAILURE TO FOLLOW THROUGH past the node; tape stalls; volume spikes at overshoot then dries up.
Trade: the REVERSION back through the node after the overshoot exhausts. Enter on early signs of reversion, wait for confirmation.

## The Whipsaw Setup (cross-index conflict)
NOT directional. The map gives CONFLICTING signals ACROSS INDICES: SPX bullish, QQQ bearish, SPY pinned. Ranges exist per index but no unified signal; structural edges cancel at macro level.
Fake moves are the norm; rapid reversals trap both sides. Edge = fade the EXTREME ends of ranges on the MOST conflicted index, ideally with a FRESH node at the fade point. Midrange = no edge.

## Rainbow Road (= don't trade)
No structure, no config, pure chaos. Random scatter, no regime, no cluster/polarity to anchor. Answer: DO NOT TRADE. Often around macro events/earnings/geopolitical — old map obsolete, new one not formed.

## Patterns in Context
Every setup interacts with the gamma regime it sits in, the magnitude of nodes, and confluence with chart structure. "Patterns are the vocabulary. Magnitude, regime, and confluence are the grammar."
